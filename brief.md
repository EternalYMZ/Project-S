# Project S: reading the proofs

Sort [2,0,4,1,3] with gap 2. The even slots change from [2,4,3] to [2,3,4]; the odd slots already form an increasing chain. One successful insertion shift moves two keys a net distance of two physical slots each. The key 0 still lies to the right of 2. These observations measure insertion work, physical displacement, and residual disorder, respectively.

To follow a key, color all smaller keys. Sorting a chain packs its colored keys into a prefix of known length. The next pass reads these prefixes through a different partition of the slots. Final ranks are available to the analyst; the running algorithm uses ordinary linear insertion.

For uniform distinct keys, the best three-pass shifts and comparison means, optimized separately, share a leading term $(\kappa+o(1))n^{3/2}$ with $0.60822153\le\kappa<0.60827485406$. Their optimal gap families are characterized by eventual coprimality and convergence in distance to a compact set of shapes. P04 lets the pass count grow. Averaging over gaps at each scale gives a single gap shared by all inputs at that size and controls the mean squared displacement. Together with the comparison-tree lower bound this yields an optimal mean comparison count of order $n\log n$. Efficient generation of an exact optimizer is a separate question.

P08 studies a different implementation: given the target records, the minimum number of whole-record writes is the number of changed slots summed over consecutive checkpoints. Other entry points include four-pass bounds (P03), ordered partitions (P06), table selection (P09), and exact finite optimization (P10).
